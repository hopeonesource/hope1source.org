## Reported results and their scope

The PDF's third page reports:

- 90% provided a cell phone number for follow-up. This is not a successful-contact rate.
- All participants interested in SNAP who had a cell phone were contacted for support. This is not a benefit-enrollment rate.
- Reporting time fell 95%, with the saved time used for direct service. This is not an overall staffing-cost reduction.
- 31% were identified as experiencing housing instability and connected with support. This is not a housing-placement or housing-resolution rate; the report does not provide sample sizes or additional denominator detail.

The second page describes one veteran whose check-in surfaced food-benefit and housing needs. Food assistance freed money for other bills, and the veteran later reported no longer experiencing housing instability. The new narrative summarizes this public, anonymous account without adding identifying or clinical details or generalizing the outcome to every participant.

## Independent coverage and network context

- [VA News: Free produce for Veterans at Charleston VA](https://news.va.gov/81753/free-produce-veterans-charleston-va/), December 6, 2020. Reports online registration, text reminders, prompts about food benefits and housing, and social-worker follow-up. Its Fresh Xpress event served 181 veterans with 280 boxes totaling more than 5,600 pounds of produce. These event figures are distinct from the PDF pilot percentages.
- [Feeding America's Lowcountry Food Bank listing](https://www.feedingamerica.org/find-your-local-foodbank/lowcountry-food-bank) confirms network membership. VA News identifies the local food-bank partnership.
